How SoSDT works

The Dolphin Sonar Journey

An experimental protocol in four stages: chlorophyll is received, a hyperbaric session floods the tissues with oxygen, the SDT bath awakens that chlorophyll with low-intensity ultrasound in a whole-body immersion tank, and the sacred atmosphere of the estate holds the work — as setting, not as the treatment itself.

SCENE I · SENSITIZER

Chlorophyll Is Received

First, chlorophyll — a light- and sound-sensitive sensitizer — is received as a small tablet dissolved under the tongue, or as a gentle IV infusion, depending on your protocol. Like light carried on a river, it travels toward the cells while you rest in stillness, sit in practice, or walk the turtle beach.

The Raw-Material Flood

Then the hyperbaric session. Under gentle pressure, blood and tissue dissolve more oxygen than ordinary breathing can carry. Tumors often live in hypoxia; this stage fills the well that the sound will later use.

In people, this is still being studied.

The Evidence

What the research says

We want you to see the evidence as it really stands, with each study labeled for what it is.

Where SDT stands today

SDT is experimental. Some early numbers look promising.

No published study yet reports results in people with liver cancer treated by SDT alone. The strongest results so far come from mice, and the human reports are small and were given alongside other treatments.

In the laboratory

Mouse and cell studies

Mouse study In mice with colorectal cancer that had spread to the liver, a chlorophyll-derived sensitizer (C34) plus ultrasound left tumor in 2.2% of the liver, against 78.6% in untreated mice. The same work found more dendritic cells and CD8 T cells, the immune cells that recognise and attack tumors. These are the immune findings shown in the SoSDT Precision Longevity brochure[1].

A 2025 follow-up paired an intravenous phytochlorin sensitizer with a dedicated free-field ultrasound system. In a mouse breast-cancer model it slowed tumor growth and stirred an immune response[2]. Dr. Wang's own early work showed SDT shrinking sarcomas in mice[3]. Mouse results often do not carry over to people.

In people

Small reports, alongside other care

Dr. Wang's team has published small, uncontrolled human reports in breast, gastric and pancreatic cancer[4, 5, 8, 9]. In each, most or all patients also received chemotherapy or other treatments, so the results cannot be credited to SDT alone. Larger, controlled trials are still needed.

Outside cancer, the best human SDT evidence comes from arteries. In a randomised, sham-controlled, double-blind trial in 32 people with leg-artery disease, SDT calmed plaque inflammation[10].

Ageing & the prostate

Senescent cells, the “zombies”

Some damaged cells stop dividing but refuse to die. These senescent cells linger and leak inflammatory signals that age the tissue around them. Clearing them delayed age-related decline in mice[11], and a first small human study of a drug that removes them lowered their numbers[12]. No SDT study of senescent cells has been published yet.

For prostate enlargement, SDT shrank the prostate in rats[13]. No human SDT trial for prostate enlargement has been published.

Other voices

A published critique

In 2010, three photodynamic-therapy specialists, Huang, Moseley and Bown, published a critique of Dr. Wang's 2009 breast report. They argued that safety and efficacy data were missing before the treatment was used in people, and they doubted that enough ultrasound reaches deep tumors[6]. Dr. Wang's team replied with their safety experience[7]. We include both, because you deserve the whole conversation.

HBOT + SoSDT

The flood, and the targeting.

Sonodynamic therapy and hyperbaric oxygen share one chemistry: singlet oxygen, a brief, luminous form of the air we breathe. Placed in sequence, they form a single rationale — first the raw-material flood, then the targeting system. This is why the hyperbaric session is the second stage of the protocol, just before the SDT bath. It is a rationale, not a proven result, and not a promise of outcome.

Stage II · The flood

Hyperbaric oxygen

Under gentle pressure, the blood and tissues dissolve more oxygen than ordinary breathing can carry. This is the raw-material flood. Many tumors live in hypoxia, a low-oxygen dark; more dissolved oxygen can mean more of the element the sound will later use, where it is aimed.

Stage III · The targeting

The SDT bath

Chlorophyll, the sensitizer, has already gathered in the body's most vulnerable cells. In a whole-body immersion tank, low-intensity, non-thermal ultrasound awakens it. Where the sound converges, oxygen is thought to be converted into reactive oxygen species, chiefly singlet oxygen, inside tumor cells. Hyperbaric oxygen fills the well. The ultrasound aims the spark. That is the theory; human evidence is still early.

The timing rule

After the session, not before.

High-dose antioxidants can quench the very oxygen species a session is meant to awaken. Please do not take them in the couple of hours before an SDT or hyperbaric session. Once the work is done, they become allies: front-load the stack afterward, so the body can settle. Ask your care team about doses and timing for your own protocol.

The stack I use

Five companions, after the echo.

Vitamin C

A familiar, water-bright antioxidant, taken after the session.

NAC

N-acetylcysteine, which feeds glutathione, the body's own deep reserve.

Alpha-lipoic acid

A versatile companion that works in both water and fat.

CoQ10

Close to the mitochondrial lamps, where the cell keeps its fire.

Astaxanthin & Lycopene

Carotenoids among the strongest quenchers of singlet oxygen.

Ask your care team about the doses that belong in your own plan.

FAQ

Honest answers

What is SoSDT?

An early-stage, experimental sonodynamic therapy protocol in four stages. First, chlorophyll — the sensitizer — is taken under the tongue or by IV, and gathers in the body's most vulnerable cells. Second, a hyperbaric oxygen session floods the tissues with dissolved oxygen, the raw material. Third, the SDT bath: you enter a whole-body immersion tank, where low-intensity, non-thermal ultrasound is meant to awaken the chlorophyll and convert oxygen into reactive oxygen species, chiefly singlet oxygen, inside tumor cells. Fourth, adjuncts — sacred design, the dolphin-sonar image, and the ancient sound of the lineages — hold a contemplative atmosphere; they are not the treatment itself. The ultrasound is quiet and non-invasive.

Should this replace current treatment?

No. This is an early-stage, experimental protocol, and I’m not recommending it to anyone. Anyone curious should keep their own doctors at the center of their care and talk it through with them.

How is the sensitizer given?

As chlorophyll, taken under the tongue or by IV, depending on your plan.

How do hyperbaric oxygen and SoSDT work together?

They share the same oxidative chemistry, singlet oxygen. Hyperbaric oxygen is the raw-material flood; the SDT bath is the targeting system. Tumors are often hypoxic, so more dissolved oxygen can mean more of that chemistry where the sound is aimed. Read the full pairing, and the antioxidant timing rule.

Should I take antioxidants around sessions?

Please do not take high-dose antioxidants in the couple of hours before an SDT or hyperbaric session; they can quench the oxygen species the session is meant to awaken. Front-load them afterward. The stack I use is vitamin C, NAC (which feeds glutathione), alpha-lipoic acid, CoQ10, and the carotenoids astaxanthin and lycopene. It’s my own experiment; anyone curious should ask their own doctor about doses. See the oxygen and sound section.
