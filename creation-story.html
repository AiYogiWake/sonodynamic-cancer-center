The Creation Story

A doctor who listened to sound, and a lineage that listens to the heart.

How a doctor's long work with sound met a lineage of Dzogchen practice, told as the people involved tell it.

Chapter I

The physician

Prof. Dr. Xiaohuai Wang, M.D., has spent more than forty years in clinical oncology. He earned his master's and medical degrees at the Fourth Military Medical University in Xi'an, was a Visiting Scholar at MD Anderson, and directed the Oncology Department of the Guangzhou Military Hospital.

Along the way he kept asking one quiet question: could cancer care be powerful and gentle at the same time? He has now retired. The work continues in the hands of those who carried it with him, and who carry it still.

Four papers on sonodynamic and photodynamic therapy are indexed under his name on PubMed (2008–2010). Further clinical reports appear in the Journal of Clinical Oncology, and he is an inventor on U.S. patents for porphyrin- and chlorin-based sonodynamic therapy. The selected bibliography gathers the links.

Chapter II

A daughter, a father, and his best friend

Dr. Lucy Qing Li, PhD, was born into medicine. She is a second-generation doctor, the daughter of a doctor who spent his life in the care of others — and who, when illness came for him, was loved by a friend who would not look away.

Her father had bladder cancer.

His best friend was Prof. Dr. Xiaohuai Wang, an oncologist who had given his working life to refining light-based therapy: photobiomodulation, the careful use of light to reach tissue that was struggling. For years he had been trying to make that work gentler, more complete. Then the person in need was not a case in a corridor. The person was his closest friend.

They would have turned the problem over the way old friends do, at a table at the end of a long day. Light could help, but light has limits. To awaken a sensitizer with light often meant an intravenous laser, or a red-light probe — a narrow path into the body. Dr. Wang had another idea, born of devotion as much as of science. Sound can travel where a beam of light cannot. Like a dolphin seeing through dark water, ultrasound can move through the whole of a person, immersively and deeply, with no probe and no laser. If a sensitizer could be received, sound might awaken it everywhere it had gone.

They took the work a step further.

They worked with a chlorophyll-based sensitizer, Sonoflora 1, which the published papers credit to the late Dr. Donald Burke of Boston, and with Dr. Doug Mitchell, a co-author on the early papers. The aim was a sensitizer without the month-long sun sensitivity that earlier chlorophyll photosensitizers had caused. A person could receive the chlorophyll, then enter a whole-body immersion tank, where low-intensity, non-thermal ultrasound is meant to awaken it — the SDT bath at the heart of this experimental protocol. It is not a meditative sound bath. It is a quiet, precise listening inside the body itself.

Lucy was a daughter in those years: watching her father, and watching the two men refuse to leave one another in the dark. Dr. Burke later passed away, and so did Lucy's father. In the aftermath, as the family tells it, the formula itself was lost.

What remained was a promise, and a daughter who had seen it made.

The next twenty years were given to both labors at once: building the machine, and re-creating the lost sensitizer — long nights in the lab, as if the work itself were a way of keeping faith with the dead and with the living. Out of that long, faithful labor came the machine and the formula together.

The clinic counts some five hundred thousand use cases across those twenty years. That is its own unverified figure, not a published study, and it is no promise of results. What it records, at root, is a friendship.

Dr. Wang has now retired. Dr. Lucy Qing Li, PhD, clinical medical scientist and CEO, carries the work forward as her life's mission: a second-generation doctor keeping faith with her father, and with the friend who would not let him face the dark alone.

She carries it now. The sound still travels.

Chapter III

The meeting

Healing traditions find one another. AiYogi, AiYogiLAB and ARDTiLYS were built around a simple Dzogchen insight: the mind rests best when the body is held in kindness. When that path crossed Dr. Wang's, both sides recognized the same intention. One brought the science of sound; the other brought meditation and a lineage of compassionate care. Hypothesis · not yet tested

Theoretical Horizon: a hypothesis under exploration

We like to wonder out loud. What follows is not a treatment claim. It is a question we hope research will one day answer.

Could gentle SDT help mitochondria renew themselves?

We imagine that the brief, controlled burst of reactive oxygen species (ROS) produced during SDT might act as a hormetic signal for healthy cells, a small challenge that invites a stronger response. Basic science shows that mild ROS can trigger protective adaptation ("mitohormesis")[12, 13], and that the master regulator PGC-1α drives mitochondrial biogenesis and antioxidant defenses[10, 11].

…and could renewed mitochondria brighten the endocrine constellation?

Every steroid hormone begins in the mitochondria: the first step of steroidogenesis, cholesterol to pregnenolone, happens on the inner mitochondrial membrane[15]. NAD+ and cellular energy decline with age[14], and cells clear worn mitochondria through mitophagy[16], a process that ROS help regulate[17]. We wonder whether a cascade from cleaner, more numerous mitochondria to steadier hormone production could one day be measured.

SignalA brief, gentle ROS pulse during SDTHormesis [12, 13]
RenewalMitophagy clears worn mitochondria[16, 17]
BiogenesisPGC-1α builds new mitochondria[10, 11]
EnergyNAD+ / ATP restored[14]
HormonesMitochondrial steroidogenesis[15]
Please read this as a hypothesis. No study has yet tested whether SDT rejuvenates mitochondria or improves endocrine function. The references describe the background biology only. We share this horizon because it inspires our research questions, not as a promise of benefit.
