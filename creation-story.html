The Creation Story

A doctor who listened to sound, and a lineage that listens to the heart.

How four decades of oncology met a Dzogchen sanctuary on Florida's Gulf Coast, and how a pod of healing centers began to swim across the world.

Chapter I

The physician

Prof. Dr. Xiaohuai Wang has spent more than forty years in clinical oncology. He earned his master's and medical degrees at the Fourth Military Medical University in Xi'an, was a Visiting Scholar at MD Anderson, and directed the Oncology Department of the Guangzhou Military Hospital.

Along the way he kept asking one quiet question: could cancer care be powerful and gentle at the same time? His answer became a life's work in sonodynamic therapy. A light- and sound-sensitive compound gathers in the body, and soft, low-intensity ultrasound awakens it. Dr. Wang went on to invent the SDT ultrasound bath used in his clinics. Today he leads SoSDT 4 LIFE in Guangzhou, China, where his team has practised SDT for more than a decade.

Chapter II

The meeting

Healing traditions find one another. AiYogi, AiYogiLAB and ARDTiLYS were built around a simple Dzogchen insight: the mind rests best when the body is held in kindness. When that path crossed Dr. Wang's, both sides recognized the same intention. One brought the science of sound; the other brought the sanctuary, the meditation, and a lineage of compassionate care. Out of that meeting came the Sonodynamic Cancer Center at AiYogi.ai Lab: Dr. Wang's protocol in a Dzogchen Florida recovery sanctuary on Manasota Key, offered free of charge to monastics.

Chapter III

The pod

Dolphins never travel alone. Our vision is a worldwide family of SDT centers that share one protocol, one standard of tenderness, and one sound, each rooted in its own landscape. Jody, our lead concierge, is helping that pod grow from shore to shore.

In Japan, Yumita, Umemura and colleagues report that hematoporphyrin, a light-sensitizing compound, also makes cells sensitive to ultrasound. The word "sonodynamic" is born.

Hypothesis · not yet tested

Theoretical Horizon: a hypothesis under exploration

We like to wonder out loud. What follows is not a treatment claim. It is a question we hope research will one day answer.

Could gentle SDT help mitochondria renew themselves?

We imagine that the brief, controlled burst of reactive oxygen species (ROS) produced during SDT might act as a hormetic signal for healthy cells, a small challenge that invites a stronger response. Basic science shows that mild ROS can trigger protective adaptation ("mitohormesis")[12, 13], and that the master regulator PGC-1α drives mitochondrial biogenesis and antioxidant defenses[10, 11].

…and could renewed mitochondria brighten the endocrine constellation?

Every steroid hormone begins in the mitochondria: the first step of steroidogenesis, cholesterol to pregnenolone, happens on the inner mitochondrial membrane[15]. NAD+ and cellular energy decline with age[14], and cells clear worn mitochondria through mitophagy[16], a process that ROS help regulate[17]. We wonder whether a cascade from cleaner, more numerous mitochondria to steadier hormone production could one day be measured.

SignalA brief, gentle ROS pulse during SDTHormesis [12, 13]
RenewalMitophagy clears worn mitochondria[16, 17]
BiogenesisPGC-1α builds new mitochondria[10, 11]
EnergyNAD+ / ATP restored[14]
HormonesMitochondrial steroidogenesis[15]
Please read this as a hypothesis. No study has yet tested whether SDT rejuvenates mitochondria or improves endocrine function. The references describe the background biology only. We share this horizon because it inspires our research questions, not as a promise of benefit.
